Learn tarot in a few minutes

This page gathers everything you need to play a game of Gothic Tarot: the number of cards for each player count, the rules specific to the gothic variant, the value of every card and the points required depending on the Bouts. Keep it handy during your first games on the Android mobile app.

1. Variations by Player Count (3, 4, 5 Players)

  • 3 Players: 24 cards per player, 6 in the dog. Each player plays for themselves against the other two.
  • 4 Players: 18 cards dealt per player, 6 in the dog. Classic solo play against the defense.
  • 5 Players (Gothic Specificity): 15 cards per player, 3 in the dog. The taker calls a king of a specific suit. The player holding this king becomes their secret partner for the round.

2. Gothic Variant Specific Rules

  • Called Suit Rule: The suit of the called king cannot be played on the first trick, unless the player leading holds and directly plays that king.
  • Excuse on the last trick: If the Excuse is played on the last trick, it goes to the side that wins that trick. Exception: during a slam, the Excuse played on the last trick by the side making the slam wins that trick.
  • Slam (chelem): winning every trick of the hand. A slam can be announced during the bidding.

4. Contracts and Required Bouts

The taker must reach a specific point threshold depending on the Bouts (1, 21, Excuse) captured:

  • With 3 Bouts: 36 points minimum required.
  • With 2 Bouts: 41 points minimum required.
  • With 1 Bout: 51 points minimum required.
  • With 0 Bouts: 56 points minimum required.

5. Contracts: Petite, Garde, Garde sans, Garde contre

During the bidding, each player passes or announces a contract higher than the previous one. The higher the contract, the more the hand is worth (or costs):

6. How a round unfolds

  1. Deal: the cards are dealt and a few are set aside to form the dog.
  2. Bidding: each player says whether they want to take; the highest bid decides the taker.
  3. The dog: depending on the contract, the taker adds the dog to their hand and then discards the same number of cards.
  4. Card play: you must follow the suit led; if you cannot, you must trump, and overtrump whenever possible.
  5. Scoring: at the end of the round, the taker's tricks are counted and compared with the threshold set by their number of Bouts.

7. How to count points in tarot

At the end of the hand, add up the points in the taker's tricks (and their partner's at 5 players), discard included. Compare this total with the target set by the number of Bouts won: the difference is called the margin.

  • Formula: hand value = (25 + margin) × contract multiplier. The taker wins this value from each defender if they succeed, or pays it if they fail.
  • 3 or 4 players: the taker receives (or pays) the hand value once per opponent.
  • 5 players: each defender pays (or receives) the hand value; the taker receives twice the value, their partner once. If the taker called their own king, they play alone against four.

Example with 4 players: the taker bids a Garde, wins 2 Bouts and scores 47 points. With 2 Bouts they needed 41: the margin is 6. The hand is worth (25 + 6) × 2 = 62 points. Each of the three defenders loses 62 points and the taker wins 186.

In Gothic Tarot, the app does this calculation for you and shows it on the score sheet at the end of each hand.

8. Petit au bout, handfuls and bonuses

  • Petit au bout: the side that wins the last trick with the Petit (the 1 of trumps) earns a 10-point bonus, multiplied by the contract. It applies even if that side loses the hand.
  • Handful (poignée): a player holding many trumps may show them before playing their first card. In Gothic Tarot the bonus is 20 points for a single handful, 30 for a double and 50 for a triple. It is not multiplied by the contract and goes to the side that wins the hand, even if the other side showed it. The app displays an “Announce handful” button as soon as your hand qualifies.

Frequently asked questions about tarot rules

How many cards does each player get in tarot?

With 3 players, each gets 24 cards and 6 cards form the dog. With 4 players, 18 cards each and 6 in the dog. With 5 players, 15 cards each and 3 in the dog.

How do you count points in tarot?

Add up the points in the taker's tricks and compare them with the target set by their Bouts (56, 51, 41 or 36 points). The hand is then worth 25 points plus the margin, multiplied by the contract: 1 for a Petite, 2 for a Garde, 4 for a Garde sans and 6 for a Garde contre.

What is the petit au bout?

It means winning the last trick with the Petit, the 1 of trumps. The side that does it earns a 10-point bonus multiplied by the contract, even if it loses the hand.

How do you play tarot with 5 players?

With 5 players, each gets 15 cards and the dog has 3 cards. The taker calls a king: the player holding it becomes their secret partner. The taker wins or loses twice the hand value, their partner once.
