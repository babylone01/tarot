How to play Gothic Tarot

Gothic Tarot follows traditional French tarot: 78 cards, bidding at the start of each round, then a taker who takes on the defence trick after trick. The idea takes minutes to grasp and many games to master: make your contract by winning the right cards at the right time.

The game counts the points for you and shows the score table at the end of every round, so you can focus on strategy rather than arithmetic.

Card values, Bouts and the points needed for each contract are all detailed on the official French tarot rules page.

Tarot for 3, 4 and 5 players

  • 3 players: a fast-paced game where everyone plays for themselves. Each player receives 24 cards and 6 cards form the dog.
  • 4 players: the reference format: 18 cards per player, 6 in the dog, and a lone taker against three defenders.
  • 5 players: each player gets 15 cards and 3 cards form the dog (kitty). The taker calls a king. The player holding it becomes their secret partner for the round, and the others must unmask them before the called king is played.

Whatever the number of players, the taker must reach a score that depends on the Bouts (the Petit, the 21 of trumps and the Excuse) they win: 56 points with no Bout, 51 with one, 41 with two and 36 with all three. Empty seats at a table are filled by the AI, so you can play with 3, 4 or 5 even on your own.

The gothic variant

Gothic Tarot applies a specific rule for the called king: on the first trick, its suit can only be led with the king itself. It is explained in full with the game rules.

An AI that remembers how you play

In Gothic Tarot, the artificial intelligence fills the empty seats at a table: it bids, calls a king with 5 players, plays its cards and counts points like a human player. It never makes an illegal move.

  • Memory of the deal: it remembers every card played, notes which suits each player is missing and knows who has run out of trumps.
  • Probabilities on every trick: it estimates the risk of being trumped and its chances of winning the trick before choosing a card.
  • Handling the Petit: with plenty of trumps, it tries to bring it home on the last trick; otherwise it saves it as soon as a safe trick comes up.
  • Your habits: from one deal to the next, it tracks ten habits per player, such as drawing trumps early or loading the taker's tricks. These profiles stay on your phone.
  • A 7% human factor: it sometimes varies its play, only on cards that do not matter, without ever giving away points.

In the weekend tournament, the bots' level adapts to your category: they play 30% random moves in the Green category, 12% in Yellow and none in Red. All the details are on the page how the game's AI works.
