A teaching AI that explains every trick

During a guided game, the recommended card blinks in your hand, with its reason in one sentence. You stay free: if you play something else, a bubble explains what that choice changes for that very card, such as points given away or an exposed Petit.

After each trick, the count comes straight from the game engine. When your choice differs from the advice, the School can also show what the recommended card would have produced, with the other cards of the trick unchanged. This AI relies on precise playing rules: it does not replace a teacher, but it is patient and always available.

The cards of French tarot

French tarot uses 78 cards: four suits of 14 cards (1 to 10, then Jack, Knight, Queen, King), 21 numbered trumps and the Excuse. Three cards rule the game: the Petit (the 1 of trumps), the 21 of trumps and the Excuse. They are the Bouts, and how many you win sets the points you need.

Bidding and contracts

Each player passes or announces a contract higher than the previous one. The highest bidder becomes the taker and plays against the defence.

In a Petite or a Garde, the taker adds the dog to their hand and then discards the same number of cards: no King, no Bout, and a trump only when there is no other choice. The discard counts towards the taker’s points.

Playing the tricks: follow, trump, go higher

  • Follow the suit led if you have it.
  • Otherwise, play a trump.
  • When trumps are played, go above the highest one on the table whenever you can.
  • With neither the suit nor a trump, play any card you like.

The game only accepts legal cards, which is a welcome safety net for beginners.

The Excuse, right up to the last trick

You can play the Excuse almost whenever you want, even when you hold the suit led. It does not win the trick, but it stays with the side that played it. In Tarot Gothique, no card is given in exchange.

Beware of the last trick: played there, the Excuse goes to the side that wins the trick. Keeping it too long can therefore cost you a Bout and 4.5 points. There is one exception: a side that has won all the other tricks also wins this one by playing the Excuse, and completes its slam.

The deal is worth (25 + difference from the target) × the multiplier. Example with 4 players: a Garde, 2 Bouts and 47 points. The target was 41, so the difference is 6 and (25 + 6) × 2 = 62. Each defender loses 62 points and the taker wins 186.

Poignées, Petit au bout and slam

  • Petit au bout: winning the last trick with the Petit earns 10 points, multiplied by the contract.
  • Poignée (handful): showing many trumps before playing. Single 20 points, double 30, triple 40. With 4 players you need 10, 13 or 15 trumps; with 3 players, 13, 15 or 18; with 5 players, 8, 10 or 13. The bonus is not multiplied and goes to the side that wins the deal.
  • Slam (chelem): winning every trick. 400 points if announced, 200 if not, and −200 when an announced slam fails.

Tarot for 3, 4 or 5 players: which one?

With 4 players, the most classic form, the taker faces three defenders: ideal for a first game. With 3 players, hands of 24 cards are long and full of trumps, and high contracts are more frequent.

With 5 players, the taker calls a King; whoever holds it becomes their secret partner. The taker scores two shares, the partner one. By calling a King they hold themselves, the taker plays alone against four. In our game, the suit of the called King is forbidden on the first trick, unless you lead that very King.

Five common beginner mistakes

  • Taking on Kings alone. Without trumps they get trumped: look first at your Bouts and the length of your trumps.
  • Forgetting to count trumps. There are 21: following them tells you when the Petit is in danger.
  • Playing the Petit too early. Any higher trump captures it, and a Bout changes sides.
  • Feeding an uncertain trick. In defence, give your Queens and Kings when your side is sure to win.
  • Holding the Excuse out of habit. Before the last tricks, decide when to play it.

Where to start learning tarot

  1. Watch the “card values” lesson.
  2. Play one guided game as the taker, then one in defence.
  3. Try a 4-player deal against the AI, offline.
  4. Read your scores again: that is where progress comes fastest.

Tarot School: frequently asked questions

Is the Tarot School free?

Yes. Lessons and guided games cost no tokens and do not count in your statistics.

How long does it take to learn to play tarot?

The basic rules make sense after a few deals. Knowing when to take and how to defend well requires more practice.

Do I need to install an app?

No. The School opens in the browser, on a computer, tablet or phone. An Internet connection is required.

Is the Excuse always kept?

No: played on the last trick, it goes to the side that wins it, except in a slam.
